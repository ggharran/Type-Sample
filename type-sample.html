The Fox
& the Crow

A Fable by Aesop

Showcasing  Playfair Display · EB Garamond · Cinzel Decorative

A Crow, having stolen a piece of cheese from a cottage window, flew with it to a tall oak tree, intending to enjoy her prize in peace. A Fox, who spied the Crow settling upon her lofty perch, resolved at once to gain the dainty morsel for himself. “How well you look today, Miss Crow,” he began, approaching with measured steps below. “What a beautiful plumage you have! And is your voice as sweet as your appearance is charming?”

“Vanity is the enemy of wisdom—and the ally of every fox who knows how to flatter.”

The Crow, pleased beyond measure by this flattery, lifted her head and, opening her beak wide to demonstrate her finest singing, let the cheese fall to the ground. The Fox snapped it up and trotted away, remarking over his shoulder: “That will do. That was all I wanted. In exchange for your cheese I will give you a piece of advice for the future—do not trust flatterers.”

Moral: “The flatterer lives at the expense of those who will listen to him.” — Aesop, c. 620–564 BCE  © Public Domain · Project Gutenberg
